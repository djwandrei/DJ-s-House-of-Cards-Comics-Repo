Evidence field guide

Know what each result can—and cannot—say.

The workbench is designed to make an argument from observed evidence without turning a sample, rate, or range into a prediction.

Read the terms before the numbersClear labels for player cards, tables, and shared-floor views
Observed
Recorded events or reconstructed shared-floor possessions in the stated package window. Always read the sample size and coverage beside the value.
Rate per 100 possessions
A pace-neutral description of the recorded sample. It is useful for comparison, not a promise about a future game.
Coverage
Which source fields were present and usable. Missing fields stay missing; the workbench does not fill them with an estimate.
Shared floor
Players who appeared together in recorded possessions. An unseen combination remains unknown rather than being labeled good or bad chemistry.
Together / apart
A descriptive same-game split. Different teammates, opponents, and situations can explain a difference.
Descriptive 95% range
A rough uncertainty band around the observed sample, not a forecast interval or proof of a player’s true talent.

Observed evidence

Structured event statistics and reconstructed shared-floor possessions. Check attempts, minutes, and coverage before drawing a conclusion.

Outside this view

Gravity, decision speed, screen navigation, and defender assignments cannot be read directly from steals, blocks, or shot percentages.

Not a forecast

These views do not predict wins, health, a player’s career, or an untested lineup. Adjusted model coefficients stay private.
