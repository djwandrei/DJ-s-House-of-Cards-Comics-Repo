Transparent by design

Data, scoring, and limitations

The optimizer keeps its math visible and its historical data boundary explicit.

01

Traceable Basketball Reference data

Team-season totals come from Basketball Reference through the DJHC historical database and are converted to per-game values before optimization. The original 15-player course snapshot and compatible CSV imports remain available as fallbacks.

02

Comparable objective scores

Each metric is converted to a 0–100 percentile within the eligible pool, then combined using your weights. Lower turnovers receive a better ball-security score.

03

Exact constraints

The solver checks every eligible combination within a safe browser limit. Locked and excluded players, position assignments, production floors, and turnover ceilings are hard rules.

04

Honest limitations

Team-season rows show players who appeared for that team; they are not a current depth chart or injury report. Box scores do not measure chemistry, matchups, role changes, or possession context.

Rotation mode ranks the roster by equal-weight player profiles, then creates a separate 240-minute plan. Its profile totals are not a projected team box score or minute-weighted forecast.

Historical data boundary

The browser reads a scoped Supabase player pool; it does not scrape Basketball Reference. Each player is limited to the selected team stint, so a midseason trade never adds that player’s all-team aggregate to a lineup. The tool is for historical exploration, not a current roster, injury, prediction, or betting service.
