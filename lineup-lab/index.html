Advanced rules

Edit who qualifies and what the plan must include

Position requirements

Recommended is the balanced setting. It accepts this season’s listed positions and allows a verified career-only position for up to 24 role-minutes in a rotation. A player may cover more than one real-life position, but each player fills only one slot at a time. Basketball Reference does not provide exact position-minute logs.

Sample filter

A player must meet both requirements to enter the search. These filters control evidence quality; neither one assigns model minutes. Starting-five defaults are 20 regular-season games with this team and 6 MPG.

Projected team totals (optional)

For a starting five, these rules add the selected players’ per-game lines. For a rotation, they use the assigned minutes and conservative per-minute rates. They describe this model’s plan—not a forecast of one real team box score.

Rotation controls
Assign minutes to match your game plan.

The optimizer gives the best-fitting group all 240 game minutes. Better players can earn larger roles, but extra minutes gradually provide less added value so small rating gaps do not force everyone to a minimum or maximum.

Exact 240-minute plan

Game-plan optimization: past team games and total minutes never restrict the plan. The model stays inside your hard limits, protects against low-opportunity spikes, and gradually tapers marginal fit above the rotation’s average workload.

Past usage is context only. Lineup Lab does not use source totals as a minute cap, target, or scoring input.

Three safeguards work together. Complete all-team season evidence is preferred when available; otherwise the model uses a fair per-appearance fallback. Limited-opportunity rates receive a modest confidence reserve, unproven advantages move toward a same-season baseline as a role expands, and extra workload gradually adds less lineup value. Team-stint games and totals are never minute caps or targets.

Transparent by design

Data, scoring, and limitations

The optimizer keeps its math visible and its historical data boundary explicit.

01

Traceable Basketball Reference data

Team-season totals come from Basketball Reference through the DJHC historical database and are converted to per-game values before optimization. The original 15-player course snapshot and compatible CSV imports remain available as fallbacks.

02

Two scores with different jobs

Search-relative score ranks groups inside one exact search and cautiously accounts for limited evidence; compare it only with alternatives from that run. Plan Fit Index is the clearer headline: 100 equals the expected same-season NBA baseline for your selected priorities. Complete Basketball Reference OBPM/DBPM values provide a small offense/defense cross-check; they never replace your priorities. Neither score is a win probability or overall player rating.

03

Exact constraints

Rotation mode checks every eligible combination with no candidate-count cutoff; the work continues in the background until it finishes or you change the scenario. Five-player lineup mode keeps a separate browser safeguard. Locked and excluded players, position assignments, production floors, and turnover ceilings are hard rules.

04

Honest limitations

The selected team-season pool shows players who appeared for that team; it is not a current depth chart or injury report. Box scores do not directly measure chemistry, matchups, role changes, or on-court possessions. Any per-100 view is clearly labelled as an estimate.

Rotation mode gives every candidate an exact 240-minute plan. The recommended model first stabilizes each metric using its own role or attempt-volume requirement, then smoothly regresses an unproven advantage as a role expands. It also applies diminishing marginal fit above the average workload for the selected rotation size, preventing a linear score difference from automatically pushing most players to their minimum or maximum. Optional production rules keep this same minute logic while testing conservative static rates, so adding a floor does not quietly change the objective. Better players can still earn larger roles, and only your displayed minimum and maximum are hard caps. Past team usage is display context only.

OBPM and DBPM are player-season box-score estimates, not proof of five-player chemistry or lineup impact. Verified play-by-play impact can be added as a separate evidence layer when coverage exists; it is not inferred from raw plus-minus or team totals.

Historical data boundary

The browser reads a scoped Supabase player pool; it does not scrape Basketball Reference. If a player changed teams, the displayed rates use the selected team row, but the number of games or total minutes recorded for that team never lowers their proposed role. The tool is for historical exploration, not a current roster, injury, prediction, or betting service.
