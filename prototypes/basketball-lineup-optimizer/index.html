Define the rules

Choose who qualifies and what the plan must include

Position coverage

Players can cover every source-listed role. For example, a PF-C may fill a forward or center requirement, but each selected player fills only one required roster slot.

Player eligibility

Only players meeting both requirements can be selected. Recommended defaults are 20 regular-season games and 3 playoff games.

Combined player profiles (optional requirements)

In lineup mode, these rules add each selected player's historical per-game line. They describe the five-player profile; they do not forecast one team box score.

Rotation minute model
Choose the level of realism for proposed minutes.

Your strategy always ranks the roster. The default minute plan then keeps the selected group close to its recorded workload, so a tiny rate sample cannot quietly inherit a star's role.

Exact 240-minute plan

Realistic historical rotation: recorded team-stint minutes create a real capacity window for each player. The selected group must have enough observed workload to cover all 240 minutes; a small, disclosed expansion is allowed only to prove exact role coverage. Hard player limits never change.

Keep observed workload uses your strategy to choose the roster, then gives its players the closest possible version of their recorded workload. Lean into the game plan shifts minutes toward the highest-fitting profiles inside the same source-backed capacity window.

Per 36 compares production at equal playing time. Projected team totals still use each player's historical per-minute production times the proposed minutes.

When the database has total minutes and shot attempts, rate stabilization pulls smaller samples toward the same-season NBA baseline before players are ranked. It is an uncertainty guardrail, not a player-impact rating.

Every option totals 240 exact on-court role minutes. Source-listed multi-position players can split their time across compatible roles; a PF only covers center when the source lists PF-C (or another C-eligible role).

Transparent by design

Data, scoring, and limitations

The optimizer keeps its math visible and its historical data boundary explicit.

01

Traceable Basketball Reference data

Team-season totals come from Basketball Reference through the DJHC historical database and are converted to per-game values before optimization. The original 15-player course snapshot and compatible CSV imports remain available as fallbacks.

02

Pool-relative fit scoring

Each metric is converted to a 0–100 percentile within the eligible, non-excluded pool, then combined using your weights. The result is a pool-relative fit score—not a win probability or universal player rating. Lower turnovers receive a better ball-security score.

03

Exact constraints

The solver checks every eligible combination within a safe browser limit. Locked and excluded players, position assignments, production floors, and turnover ceilings are hard rules.

04

Honest limitations

Team-season rows show players who appeared for that team; they are not a current depth chart or injury report. Box scores do not directly measure chemistry, matchups, role changes, or on-court possessions. Any per-100 view is visibly labelled as a team-total-based estimate.

Rotation mode evaluates each candidate with an exact 240-minute plan under your player limits and selected on-court role shape. The recommended historical-aware plan uses recorded team-stint workload as a guardrail, compares counting production per 36, and stabilizes small samples when the source evidence is available. Open what-if mode deliberately removes the workload guardrail. Projected production still uses each player's historical per-minute production times the exact minutes assigned.

Player-season box scores and recorded workload are not observed five-player impact. Verified play-by-play impact can be added as a separate evidence layer when coverage exists; it is not inferred from raw plus-minus or team totals.

Historical data boundary

The browser reads a scoped Supabase player pool; it does not scrape Basketball Reference. Each player is limited to the selected team stint, so a midseason trade never adds that player’s all-team aggregate to a lineup. The tool is for historical exploration, not a current roster, injury, prediction, or betting service.
